Does oxidised silver turn black? Telling patina from damage

At EvaStone oxidation is a designed element, not ageing. It sits in the recesses of the surface structure and traces the pattern. What changes with wear is the patina on the raised areas — and that can be taken back. This article separates the two so you do not have to guess during a sale.

What oxidation is

Oxidation is applied deliberately in the workshop and then removed again from the raised areas. What remains sits where the structure is deep. That contrast is what makes the pattern visible — without it the surface reads as flat.

What patina is

Patina comes from wear: skin contact, cosmetics, air. It settles evenly over the raised areas and takes the shine off the piece. This is normal and reversible.

The difference in one sentence

Oxidation sits in the depth and belongs to the design. Patina lies on top and comes off with a polishing cloth.

What to tell the customer

A polishing cloth, a few strokes, only across the raised areas. No dip baths and no household silver polish: both attack the recesses too and take away exactly what the piece was bought for.
